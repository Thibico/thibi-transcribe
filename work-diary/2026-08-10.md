## `bc90d42` — core primitives, the ObjectStore port, the database schema

Three of the six packages.

**`packages/core`** gains the wire-neutral transcript types, timecode, the no-words
interpolation fallback and `resolveLayer`. Two decisions worth recording:

- `interpolateWords` takes a minimal structural `SegmentationRules` rather than the
  `ResolvedLanguage` the plan specified. `core` may depend on nothing — that is the ESLint
  layer rule from Phase 0 — and it must stay importable from a React client component. The
  caller passes the two fields that matter. A small deviation, and the alternative was
  either breaking the layer rule or duplicating the function.
- `formatTimestamp` works in integer milliseconds and rounds exactly once, killing
  [overview amendment 3](../plans/00-overview.md) at the root. The old `lib/export.ts:15-22`
  turned 59.9996 s into `00:00:59,1000` — a four-digit millisecond field and malformed SRT —
  because the rounding never carried into the seconds. The regression test asserts that
  input and the full 23:59:59.9996 cascade.

**`packages/storage`** is one port, three adapters, one `describe.each` contract suite. That
is what makes `STORAGE_DRIVER=fs` — the overview's Risk-4 cut list — a supported
configuration rather than a degraded one, and it lets everything above this layer test
without a container. `toTempFile` hardlinks when the store is fs-backed on the same device,
and its directory is gone after both a normal return and a throw, which is the whole reason
for `await using` over a `finally`.

**`packages/db`** is the schema, our own migration runner and `copyWords`. The generated
`0000_init.sql` has 11 tables with the partial unique indexes and CHECK constraints intact.
The load-bearing details: `segment_texts.target_lang` is NOT NULL defaulting to `''`,
because a partial unique index over a NULLable column enforces nothing (`NULL <> NULL`) and
a test that only inserts translations would never notice; `words.id` is a bigint identity
rather than a uuid, because 10M rows at 1,000 audio-hours makes a random primary key double
the index and destroy COPY's insert locality; and the migration runner is ours rather than
drizzle-kit's because [Phase 15](../plans/phase-15-deployment.md) runs it as a compose
one-shot with no dev tooling in the image.

## `9623281` and `94417cd` — engine core, audio stages, seam merge, Google provider

82 tests. The seam merge is the phase's real algorithm.

Chunk *k+1* is extracted 1200 ms before chunk *k* ends, so its transcript repeats the tail
of chunk *k* — and spike S3 measured that hard cuts lose 2–3 words at every seam, 2.1% of
all words at 30 minutes and 3.4% at 2 hours. LCS over a time-bounded window de-duplicates
it. Below a Dice score of 0.5 it hard-cuts at the overlap midpoint and flags the seam,
because a duplicated sentence reads as the speaker repeating themselves, which is worse for
a reader than a missing one. Unspaced scripts align on graphemes, since provider "words" in
Burmese are syllable fragments that do not tokenise the same way twice. An empty overlap
scores 1 by convention rather than NaN — a low score there would flag every pause in a
recording.

Three things measured while building, each of which changed the code:

- **A lead at or above the chunk maximum** drove the planning window to nothing and produced
  thousands of one-millisecond chunks. `--overlap-ms` is user input, so it clamps to half
  the maximum rather than failing obscurely.
- **Zawgyi conversion is byte-identical per word and whole-string**, because the reordering
  it does never crosses a word boundary. That is the property per-word conversion depends
  on, so it is asserted rather than assumed. My first attempt asserted the conversion was
  not length-preserving — which is not true for real Burmese, and was the wrong
  justification anyway. The right one: converting segment text in place leaves the word
  array in Zawgyi and the two disagreeing, and the alignment cannot be recovered by
  re-splitting because the transform reorders within syllables.
- **The token cache renews at 44 minutes, not 45** — a 45-minute recorded lifetime with a
  60-second guard. The guard is what stops a request starting just under the wire from being
  the one that fails.

`google/parse.ts` is where the word array becomes the *output* rather than a source of
bounds; `google.ts:207-221` read the first and last offsets and threw the array away.
`google/errors.ts` ports `toError` with `:139-141` deleted — the hint that appended "check
the region: Chirp 2 and Burmese only overlap in asia-southeast1 and europe-west4" to every
INVALID_ARGUMENT naming a model or language. It is a false statement that sent operators to
re-check a setting that was never the problem, and a test now asserts no message this module
produces names a region.

`capabilities.ts` carries the S1 and S2 verdicts as literals with their dates, so
`adaptation: 'none'` traces to [the spike](../spikes/RESULTS.md) that found an irrelevant
phrase set corrupting five occurrences of a word the baseline got right.

The second commit is a correction: lint caught an unused `minScore` in the no-words seam
branch, which was a real gap rather than a style nit. Thinking it through, the score
*cannot* change the decision at segment granularity — the overlap region contains audio we
extracted twice, so something was said twice whether or not the two transcriptions of it
agree. The score is now documented as informational, and what it would take to act on it is
written down.

## `d5ec420` — the pipeline and the CLI

`thibi transcribe` runs the whole slice. Verified on a real 100-second Burmese file: 2
chunks, 197 words with full timings and per-word confidence, 13 seconds, $0.027.

**Running it on real audio found a bug no unit test had.** The seam windowed prev-side words
by `endMs`, so a 760 ms word that *began* before the overlap was included — audio chunk
*k+1* never received and therefore can never align. It dropped the score from 0.86 to 0.43
and forced a hard cut on a perfect seam. The plan's own pseudocode says `startMs`; I had
deviated. Fixed, and the file now scores 1.0.

One unit test failed on the fix, and the fixture was what was wrong: it gave chunk *k* a
word extending past its own end, which is audio that chunk does not have. Corrected to
realistic geometry, and the real-file case became its own named regression test.

**The measured benefit of the overlap, on that file:** 197 words with the merge against 196
with `--overlap-ms 0`, both reading identically at the seam. The overlap recovered a word
the hard cut would have lost and the merge removed the duplicate it introduced — exactly
what spike S3's 2–3-words-per-seam measurement predicted.

`apps/cli/src/context.ts` is the only file that reads the environment, and its key list is
exhaustive and reviewed. `--no-db` is a memory store rather than a special case threaded
through the pipeline, which is what keeps persistence a stage. Exit 4 prints the transcript.

Both CI greps now strip comment lines before matching. The rules are worth explaining where
they are enforced, and a check that forbids naming the thing it forbids would delete its own
documentation — here, the record of which false region claim was removed and why.
